Identity — For Teens

Who Told You That?

You know that moment when someone says something small — "you're too loud," "you're not smart like your sister," "you'll never make the team" — and it just... sticks? Weeks later you're still carrying it around like a name tag you didn't ask to wear.

Here's the thing nobody tells you early enough: somebody is always going to have an opinion about who you are. Your job isn't to collect all of them. Your job is to find out what's actually true.

So — who told you that you're not enough? Was it a teacher, a comment section, a mirror, a bad grade? Now ask a better question: who told you that you're fearfully and wonderfully made (Psalm 139:14)? That one's from God, and He doesn't do rough drafts.

A few identity lies that love to hang around campus and comment sections

  • "You are what people say about you."
  • "Your worth = your grades, your looks, or your likes."
  • "If you don't know who you are yet, something's wrong with you."

What's actually true

  • You were made on purpose, not by accident (Genesis 1:27).
  • Your identity was settled before your achievements started — Ephesians 2:10 says you're His workmanship, created for good works, not created by them.
  • Figuring out who you are is a process, not a test you can fail.

Real talk: identity built on grades, followers, or other people's approval will always feel shaky, because all three can change by tomorrow. Identity built on "child of God" doesn't move — no matter what your report card, your relationship status, or your comment section says this week.

"I praise you because I am fearfully and wonderfully made." — Psalm 139:14

Try this: next time a label tries to stick, ask — "Did God say that about me?" If not, peel it off. You don't have to wear everything people hand you.

You are not your worst day, your last mistake, or your lowest follower count. You are His. Start there.

Identity — For Young Adults

The Job Title Isn't You

Somewhere between graduation and now, a question started following you around: "So what do you do?" And somewhere along the way, without quite noticing, you started answering it as if it were the same question as "So who are you?"

It isn't. But try telling that to a room full of twenty-somethings comparing job titles, relationship statuses, and how "put together" their lives look on paper.

Here's what nobody warns you about this season of life: it's when identity gets outsourced the fastest. To a job you haven't landed yet. To a relationship you're not in yet. To a version of "adulting" you're convinced everyone else has figured out. And when any of those wobble — layoff, breakup, another year unmarried, a business that hasn't taken off — it can feel like you are falling apart, not just your circumstances.

A few identity traps that love this exact age bracket

  • "I'll know who I am once I have the job/marriage/house."
  • "Everyone else clearly has it figured out — I'm behind."
  • "My worth is directly tied to my productivity right now."

By the way, reputation is not synonymous to identity

  • o Reputation lives outside you — in other people's heads. Identity lives inside you. You can't control one. You can build the other.
  • o The problem isn't that reputation matters. It does. The problem is when you start deriving your identity from your reputation — when who you are depends on what people think of you. That's a very unstable place to live.
  • Reputation is what you've done and what others have said. Identity is who you actually are. Today we're working on the second one.

What's actually true

  • Ephesians 2:10 says you are God's workmanship, created for good works — your identity was settled before your career was.
  • Colossians 3:3 says your real life is "hidden with Christ" — secure even when your resume, relationship status, or bank account is in flux.
  • Comparison is a job with no retirement plan. It will never stop giving you new people to measure yourself against.

Real talk: a job title can be taken away in a single meeting. A relationship can end in a single conversation. If either one is holding up your sense of self, you're building on sand. Identity anchored in "chosen, made on purpose, hidden in Christ" holds steady on the good years and the hard ones.

"For we are God's handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works." — Ephesians 2:10

Try this: write one sentence that answers "who am I" without mentioning your job, relationship status, or achievements. If that's hard to do, that's worth sitting with.

You are not behind. You are not your LinkedIn. You are His — and that was already settled.
